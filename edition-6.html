Jargon Buster

Scary 😨 Words: Tokens

Every time you use ChatGPT, Copilot, or any AI tool, your conversation is being measured in tokens. Think of tokens as the AI’s currency — and every conversation has a budget.

But here’s the thing: a token isn’t exactly a word. It’s more like a chunk of a word. Short, common words like ‘the’, ‘cat’, or ‘hello’ are usually one token each. But longer or less common words get broken up: ‘unbelievable’ might become three tokens (‘un’, ‘believ’, ‘able’), and ‘antidisestablishmentarianism’ could be seven or eight.

Think of it like a text message on an old phone. Remember when texts had a character limit and you had to be strategic about what you included? Tokens work the same way. The AI has a maximum budget for reading your input and writing its response — combined. Use up too much on a massive input, and there’s less room left for the answer.

As a rough guide, 1 token ≈ ¾ of a word in English. So 1,000 tokens is roughly 750 words — about three pages of double-spaced text.

Why should you care? This is why:

  • It’s why you can’t paste an entire textbook into ChatGPT and expect a useful answer
  • It’s why shorter, focused prompts often get better results — you’re leaving more budget for the reply
  • It’s why some AI plans cost more than others — more tokens = more expensive to run

Next time an AI tool tells you your input is too long, you’ll know exactly what’s happening: you’ve hit the token limit. The fix? Break your request into smaller chunks, or trim down what you’re pasting in.

Ditto Clipboard

📋

Ditto Clipboard

Free Clipboard Manager for Windows

“Wait, this isn’t an AI tool!” I hear you cry. You’re right. But Unity Boost isn’t just about AI — it’s about making your working life easier. And Ditto might be the single most useful free tool I’ve ever installed on a computer.

Here’s the problem we all have: you copy something, then you copy something else, and the first thing is gone forever. You end up flicking between windows, copying and pasting one thing at a time, losing things constantly. It’s maddening.

Ditto fixes this. It sits quietly in your system tray and saves everything you copy — text, images, links, the lot. When you need something back, you just open Ditto and it’s all there.

⭐ Key Features

📋 Clipboard History Automatically saves everything you copy. Need that thing from 3 hours ago? It's still there.
🔍 Instant Search Type to search through your entire clipboard history. Find anything in seconds.
📌 Pin Favourites Pin text you use all the time — feedback phrases, email sign-offs, URLs you always need.
⌨️ Quick Paste Press Ctrl+' to open Ditto, double-click any item to paste it. That's it.

🎬 Getting Started (2 minutes, genuinely)

  1. Download Ditto from ditto-cp.sourceforge.io — it’s a tiny install
  2. Run it. It sits in your system tray (bottom-right of your screen, near the clock)
  3. Copy things as normal — Ctrl+C as you always do. Ditto remembers them all
  4. Press Ctrl+’ to open your clipboard history (the video above shows how to change this to Ctrl+D if you prefer)
  5. Double-click any item to paste it. That’s it. Life changed.
🔒

Is it Safe?

Yes. Everything is stored locally on your own computer — nothing is sent to the cloud or shared anywhere. It’s open source, so anyone can inspect the code. Don’t copy passwords with it, though — that’s just good sense.

💰

Is it Free?

Completely free and open source. No ads, no trials, no premium tier. Just a generous developer who made something brilliant and gave it away.

Useful reading/listening links

Report OECD

OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026: The ‘AI Learning Paradox’

The big one. The OECD found that students produce better work with AI, but when AI access is removed (like in exams), the advantage disappears — or reverses. They call it ‘cognitive offloading’: students consume polished solutions instead of doing the thinking. Essential reading for anyone wondering how AI is really affecting learning.

Article Faculty Focus

Designing the 2026 Classroom: Emerging Learning Trends in an AI-Powered Education System

AI adoption among teachers has nearly doubled since 2023, with 60%+ now using it regularly. This piece looks at what that actually means in practice — how classrooms are changing, what’s working, and what the emerging trends look like for the year ahead. A good big-picture read.

Opinion Education Week

Train Teachers on AI? It’s Not That Simple

Teachers are on the front lines of AI adoption but most have had no formal training. This piece argues it’s not just about ‘more CPD’ — the whole approach needs rethinking. Sound familiar? (It’s basically why this newsletter exists.)
